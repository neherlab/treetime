# Resampling past a hard end moves the end out to the next grid point

`fn DistributionFunction::resample()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L167-L177](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L167-L177) evaluates the function at every point of the new grid through `fn GridFn::resample_with_extrap()` [packages/treetime-grid/src/grid_fn.rs#L379-L390](../../packages/treetime-grid/src/grid_fn.rs#L379-L390), with the boundary laws of the function. A new grid point beyond a hard end (a `Hard` side, or beyond `t_hard` of a `HardApproach` law) gets the cost `+inf`, and the resampled function keeps the old boundary laws. The resampled function does not know the old end: in a cell with one infinite and one finite sample, `fn GridFn::hard_cell()` [packages/treetime-grid/src/grid_fn.rs#L150-L171](../../packages/treetime-grid/src/grid_fn.rs#L150-L171) puts the hard bound at the infinite sample (`t_boundary`, line 164) and fits a hard-approach law from that sample to the finite samples. It reuses the stored law only in the first or last cell, and only when its `t_hard` coincides with the infinite sample. The cost between the infinite sample and the old hard end is therefore finite, and the support grows outward by up to one cell of the new grid. When the finite run next to the infinite sample has only one sample, `fn GridFn::fit_hard_cell_law()` [packages/treetime-grid/src/grid_fn.rs#L317-L334](../../packages/treetime-grid/src/grid_fn.rs#L317-L334) has no second point to fit and uses a flat law (`b = 0`, line 331), so the cost stays at the cost of that sample across the whole cell, up to the infinite sample.

Example: the function on `[1, 3]` with costs `3, 2, 1` and `Hard` sides, resampled with `resample_range_dx((0.0, 4.0), 0.5)`, stores `+inf` at 0 and 0.5, as `test_function_resample_beyond_hard_ends_has_infinite_cost` [`packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/__tests__/test_function.rs#L77-L94`](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/__tests__/test_function.rs#L77-L94) asserts. The cell `[0.5, 1]` is a hard cell with its bound at 0.5, so the resampled function has a finite cost at 0.75, where the input has `+inf`. A `HardApproach` side with `t_hard = 0.25` gives the same result in the cell `[0, 0.5]`: the stored law does not coincide with the infinite sample at 0, so the refit law puts the bound at 0 and gives a finite cost at 0.1.

The same mechanism applies inside the grid: an input with a run of infinite samples (a gap in the support) that is resampled onto a grid whose points fall into the gap puts each edge of the gap at the next infinite sample of the new grid. A new grid whose spacing exceeds the width of a gap can place no point inside it: every new sample is then finite, and the linear interpolation between them removes the gap.

## Production callers

The refit at an infinite sample needs a new grid point beyond a hard end, where `fn GridFn::resample_with_extrap()` stores `+inf`. The callers of that path keep their grids inside the hard ends of their input:

- `fn DistributionFunction::resample_range_n_points()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L179-L182](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L179-L182) has one production caller, `fn resample_to_mass_window()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/mass_domain.rs#L355](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/mass_domain.rs#L355). Its windows come from:
  - `fn rewindow_to_mass()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/mass_domain.rs#L25-L39](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/mass_domain.rs#L25-L39), called by the branch-length construction [packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L79](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L79): the window is `fn mass_bounded_domain()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/mass_domain.rs#L303-L323](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/mass_domain.rs#L303-L323), which ends every `Hard`, `HardApproach`, and `HardEdgeCell` side at the end of the grid
  - `fn WindowedConvolution::resample()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L293](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L293) and `fn ForwardConvolution::resolve_posterior()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L76](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L76): `fn WindowedConvolution::new()` refits the soft side as a `Linear` tail [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L234](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L234) and clips the window at the end of the grid on the hard side, or takes `fn mass_bounded_domain()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L258-L266](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L258-L266)
- `fn DistributionFunction::resample_range_dx()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L191-L194](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L191-L194) has no production caller; tests of `treetime-distribution` and `treetime` call it

The convolution resamples its operands through a second path, which stores samples beyond a hard end in another way. `fn DistributionFunction::resample_dx()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L214-L218](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L214-L218), called in production only by `fn on_common_spacing()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/convolve.rs#L331](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/convolve.rs#L331) for both operands of `fn convolution_function_function_fine()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/convolve.rs#L274-L275](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/convolve.rs#L274-L275), which `fn WindowedConvolution::new()` reaches through `fn distribution_convolution_fine()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L232](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/edge_convolution.rs#L232), goes through `fn GridFn::resample_range_dx_clamped()` [packages/treetime-grid/src/grid_fn.rs#L392-L406](../../packages/treetime-grid/src/grid_fn.rs#L392-L406). It clamps each query into the old grid, but stores the value at the coordinate of the new grid, and the new grid keeps only one end on an end of the old grid (`fn resample_anchor()` [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L316-L324](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_core/function.rs#L316-L324)) while its rounded point count moves the other end by up to half a cell. Example: `[0, 1]` resampled at the spacing 0.6 with a left anchor rounds $1/0.6 + 1 \approx 2.67$ to 3 points, so the new grid is 0, 0.6, 1.2, and the sample at 1.2 holds the cost of the old end at 1. When that end is hard, the result has a finite cost between 1 and 1.2. When the old end sample is `+inf`, the sample at 1.2 is `+inf` too, and the cell `[0.6, 1.2]` becomes a refit hard cell with its bound at 1.2. The anchor keeps the single hard end of an operand exact, so this path moves a hard end only for an operand with two hard ends; [N-timetree-resampled-time-distributions-with-two-hard-ends-move-one-end-by-up-to-half-a-cell.md](N-timetree-resampled-time-distributions-with-two-hard-ends-move-one-end-by-up-to-half-a-cell.md) records its occurrences and its fix options.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Investigation required.** Whether production distributions reach either path in a way that moves a hard end. Collect, per caller on the timetree smoke datasets with a temporary probe:
>
> - the inputs of `fn resample_to_mass_window()` with an interior run of infinite samples, whose gap edges move as described above
> - the operands of `fn on_common_spacing()` (`convolve.rs` line 331) with two hard ends whose resampled grid ends past the old end, and among them the operands whose end sample is `+inf`, which become a refit hard cell with its bound at the moved end

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Decision required.** The fix of the refit at an infinite sample. Options:
>
> - Keep the old end: the resampled function stores the old hard end as a boundary law of its first or last cell (a `HardApproach` law whose `t_hard` is the old end), and `fn GridFn::hard_cell()` takes the bound from that law also when it lies inside the cell
> - Clip the grid: the resampling ends the new grid at each hard end of its input, so no new sample lies beyond it; a caller that needs a fixed grid gets a shorter one
> - Reject the call: the resampling returns an error for a grid that extends past a hard end, because no production caller needs it
