# A soft tail with a slope near zero stretches a time grid far beyond the data

A `Linear` tail law with neg-log slope $k$ holds the mass $p_\text{edge} / |k|$ beyond its grid edge, where $p_\text{edge}$ is the density at the edge, and its mass quantiles lie at distances of order $\ln(1/\epsilon) / |k|$ from the edge for a mass fraction $\epsilon$. When an operation produces a decaying slope close to zero, a mass window or a mass quantile of that distribution lies years to trillions of years away from the data, and the next grid at the operand spacing exceeds the grid point limit or takes most of the run time. No code bounds the slope of a soft tail from below.

## Observed cases

- **Leaf range message** (fixed): on `data/mpox/clade-ii/1000` with `--clock-rate=1e-3 --max-iter=1`, the range convolution that shifted a function to the midpoint of a date range left a leaf message with the left slope $-4.6 \cdot 10^{-12}$; its $\epsilon$ quantile lay at $-1.66 \cdot 10^{12}$ years, and the run stopped on a grid over \[-1.66e12, 2022.62\] that needed $2.1 \cdot 10^{16}$ points. The exact range convolution ([kb/decisions/distribution-tails-and-arithmetic.md](../decisions/distribution-tails-and-arithmetic.md#exact-point-and-range-convolution)) removed this source; the run completes at the rates $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$, $7 \cdot 10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$ and $2 \cdot 10^{-3}$, with 35 nodes keeping their given date, as before at $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$
- **Forward convolution output**: on `data/mpox/clade-ii/1000` at `--clock-rate=5e-4` (2026-10-24), the forward window of leaf `OP696840` reaches \[2022.36, 2033.92\] for a posterior about 0.08 years wide, and the run completes with a largest forward grid of 43 513 points (21 758 at $10^{-3}$, 10 840 at $2 \cdot 10^{-3}$). An earlier, larger example on `data/flu/h3n2/200` at `--clock-rate=3.058e-3 --keep-root` is not evidence: that input tree is rooted on a 2007 tip, so `--keep-root` contradicts the 1968 to 1972 samples; rerooted, the largest forward grid of that dataset is 1 323 points
- **Polytomy resolution**: `data/zika/86` with `--coalescent opt --seed 7 --max-iter 6` and polytomy resolution stopped in round 2 on a grid over \[0, 2949.41\] (1 062 601 points). An empty sparse observation of a polytomy merger node caused it; the run completes since `fn reconcile_topology()` receives the merges of the resolution (`PolytomyResolution.merges`, `packages/treetime/src/partition/marginal/sparse/partition.rs`), with a largest forward grid of 1 571 points on 2026-10-24. Whether a near-flat tail carried the grid to year 0 is not shown
- **Wrong fixed `Tc` on simulated data**: timetree runs on the standard simulated cell (`fn timetree_cell()` [packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L49](../../packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L49) with the flags of `fn timetree_cell_params()` [packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L66](../../packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L66): 20 tips, constant true $T_c = 5$, true clock rate fixed) with `coalescent: Some(<wrong Tc>)`, for example 0.5 or 1.0, sometimes stop at the grid point limit. Whether a near-flat soft tail stretches the grid in these runs is not shown

  > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Investigation required.** The seeds and the frequency of the failing runs are not recorded. Run replicates of the cell with $T_c$ 0.5 and 1.0 through `fn run_replicates()` [packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L264](../../packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L264), record the failing seeds, and check the range and spacing of the grid that exceeds the limit (`GridPointLimitExceeded`)

- **Pilot grid that ended at the likelihood peak** (fixed): the Nextstrain OC43 build of seasonal-cov (`--clock-rate 0.00025 --clock-std-dev 0.0001 --coalescent opt`, marginal branch lengths) stopped in round 2 on the branch distribution of edge 733 (`NODE_0000374` to `NODE_0000379`, divergence length $5.6 \cdot 10^{-7}$), whose mass window spanned \[0, 26 180\] years at the spacing 0.00435 years, 6 015 369 points. The pilot grid spanned $[0, \max(5 b, 10 / L)]$ around the divergence length $b$, \[0, 10 mutations\] for this edge, while from round 1 on the reconstruction runs on the clock branch lengths (`fn RoundState::marginal_update()` [packages/treetime/src/timetree/round.rs#L403-L407](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/round.rs#L403-L407)) and the log-likelihood of the edge peaked at about 10.8 mutations. With indels, the indel decay of 0.55 nats per year moved the maximum two points inside the grid end, `fn SoftTailLaw::fit()` fitted the flat top of the peak with the slope $2.9 \cdot 10^{-4}$ nats per year, and its tail mass put the survival quantile at 26 180 years. Without indels, the maximum was the last grid point, the fit clamped the rising slope to flat, and 49 branch distributions of the run kept a flat right tail, 16 more a decaying slope below 1 nat per pilot width (mass windows up to 337 years). v0 holds such a peak, because for a branch this short `BranchLenInterpolator` samples 10 mutations linearly and then a quadratic grid up to `MAX_BRANCH_LENGTH` = 4.0 [packages/legacy/treetime/treetime/branch_len_interpolator.py#L38-L47](../../packages/legacy/treetime/treetime/branch_len_interpolator.py#L38-L47). `fn pilot_grid()` [packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L89](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L89) now extends the pilot grid at its own spacing until the log-likelihood has fallen $\ln(1/\epsilon)$ below its running maximum, up to `MAX_BRANCH_LENGTH` = 5 subs/site. A likelihood that does not fall that far before 5 subs/site gets a grid that ends there with a hard right edge, whether the pilot grid already reaches 5 subs/site or the extension does, so the support does not depend on the current branch length; v0 builds every branch grid up to `MAX_BRANCH_LENGTH` and gives zero probability outside it [packages/legacy/treetime/treetime/distribution.py#L295-L307](../../packages/legacy/treetime/treetime/distribution.py#L295-L307). A constant likelihood, such as one without data, keeps its pilot grid and its flat soft tail. On 2026-11-02 the run completes with and without indels, no branch distribution of either run keeps a flat or near-flat right tail, the widest branch window spans 64 years (67 without indels), and `NODE_0000379` is dated 2021.098 (2021.077 without indels)

- **Soft window clamp refuted**: clamping the soft side of a mass window to a multiple of the input grid width failed 4 tests and moved flu dates by up to 13.5 years, because the far mass is real tail mass that the FFT drops later

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Decision required.** How to guard against a soft tail whose slope is near zero. Options:
>
> - **Lower bound on the slope of a soft tail law**, for example from the width of the distribution's grid: changes the mass of the tail, which the log marginal likelihood and the posterior statistics read, and needs a rule for the bound
> - **Window cap relative to the input grid**: a mass window ends at most a fixed multiple of the operand's width from its grid; the refuted clamp above is one such rule, so a cap needs a measurement that it changes no result where the tail mass is real
> - **Error that names the node**: stop with an error that names the node and the tail slope when a mass window exceeds a bound, instead of the grid point error
>
> Evidence: the cases above. The OC43 case came from a pilot grid that ended at the likelihood peak, and a pilot grid that reaches past the peak removed it without a guard; none of the three guards fixes that cause, and an error would stop a run whose likelihood is valid. The narrow-branch case of [M-timetree-narrow-branch-distribution-forces-millions-of-grid-points.md](M-timetree-narrow-branch-distribution-forces-millions-of-grid-points.md) is a different mechanism (a fine spacing, not a wide window).

## Validation

- A test with a soft tail of slope $10^{-12}$ that enters a convolution: the run either keeps the grid below the limit with the decided guard, or stops with the decided error
- `data/mpox/clade-ii/1000` at the four rates above keeps its largest grid below 100 000 points (met on 2026-10-24, largest 43 513)
