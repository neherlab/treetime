# A soft tail with a slope near zero stretches a time grid far beyond the data

A `Linear` tail law with neg-log slope $k$ holds the mass $p_\text{edge} / |k|$ beyond its grid edge, where $p_\text{edge}$ is the density at the edge, and its mass quantiles lie at distances of order $\ln(1/\epsilon) / |k|$ from the edge for a mass fraction $\epsilon$. When an operation produces a decaying slope close to zero, a mass window or a mass quantile of that distribution lies years to trillions of years away from the data, and the next grid at the operand spacing exceeds the grid point limit or takes most of the run time. No code bounds the slope of a soft tail from below.

## Observed cases

- **Leaf range message** (fixed): on `data/mpox/clade-ii/1000` with `--clock-rate=1e-3 --max-iter=1`, the range convolution that shifted a function to the midpoint of a date range left a leaf message with the left slope $-4.6 \cdot 10^{-12}$; its $\epsilon$ quantile lay at $-1.66 \cdot 10^{12}$ years, and the run stopped on a grid over \[-1.66e12, 2022.62\] that needed $2.1 \cdot 10^{16}$ points. The exact range convolution ([kb/decisions/distribution-tails-and-arithmetic.md](../decisions/distribution-tails-and-arithmetic.md#exact-point-and-range-convolution)) removed this source; the run completes at the rates $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$, $7 \cdot 10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$ and $2 \cdot 10^{-3}$, with 35 nodes keeping their given date, as before at $5 \cdot 10^{-4}$
- **Forward convolution output**: on `data/mpox/clade-ii/1000` at `--clock-rate=5e-4` (2026-10-24), the forward window of leaf `OP696840` reaches \[2022.36, 2033.92\] for a posterior about 0.08 years wide, and the run completes with a largest forward grid of 43 513 points (21 758 at $10^{-3}$, 10 840 at $2 \cdot 10^{-3}$). An earlier, larger example on `data/flu/h3n2/200` at `--clock-rate=3.058e-3 --keep-root` is not evidence: that input tree is rooted on a 2007 tip, so `--keep-root` contradicts the 1968 to 1972 samples; rerooted, the largest forward grid of that dataset is 1 323 points
- **Polytomy resolution**: `data/zika/86` with `--coalescent opt --seed 7 --max-iter 6` and polytomy resolution stopped in round 2 on a grid over \[0, 2949.41\] (1 062 601 points). An empty sparse observation of a polytomy merger node caused it; the run completes since `fn reconcile_topology()` receives the merges of the resolution (`PolytomyResolution.merges`, `packages/treetime/src/partition/marginal/sparse/partition.rs`), with a largest forward grid of 1 571 points on 2026-10-24. Whether a near-flat tail carried the grid to year 0 is not shown
- **Wrong fixed `Tc` on simulated data**: timetree runs on the standard simulated cell (`fn timetree_cell()` [packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L49](../../packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L49) with the flags of `fn timetree_cell_params()` [packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L66](../../packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L66): 20 tips, constant true $T_c = 5$, true clock rate fixed) with `coalescent: Some(<wrong Tc>)`, for example 0.5 or 1.0, sometimes stop at the grid point limit, with a message that blames the true fixed rate ([N-timetree-grid-point-limit-error-blames-the-clock-rate.md](N-timetree-grid-point-limit-error-blames-the-clock-rate.md)). Whether a near-flat soft tail stretches the grid in these runs is not shown

  > [!IMPORTANT]
  > **Investigation required.** The seeds and the frequency of the failing runs are not recorded. Run replicates of the cell with $T_c$ 0.5 and 1.0 through `fn run_replicates()` [packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L264](../../packages/treetime/src/test_utils/simulation.rs#L264), record the failing seeds, and check the range and spacing of the grid that exceeds the limit (`GridPointLimitExceeded`)

- **Pilot grid that ends at the likelihood peak**: the Nextstrain OC43 build of seasonal-cov (`--clock-rate 0.00025 --clock-std-dev 0.0001 --coalescent opt`, marginal branch lengths) stops in round 2 while it builds the branch distribution of edge 733 (`NODE_0000374` to `NODE_0000379`, divergence length $5.6 \cdot 10^{-7}$): `fn compute_branch_length_distribution()` calls `fn rewindow_to_mass()` [packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L63](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L63), whose mass window spans \[0, 26 180\] years at the spacing 0.00435 years, 6 015 369 points. The same run finishes with `--no-indels`. Measured on 2026-11-02 with debug output of the pilot grid, the tail fit, and the likelihood beyond the grid:

  - **Pilot grid**: `fn create_simple_grid()` [packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L69-L80](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L69-L80) spans $[0, \max(5 b, 10 / L)]$ around the divergence length $b$ ([packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/runner.rs#L273](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/runner.rs#L273)); for this edge \[0, 10 mutations\] = \[0, 1.30\] years, 300 points, in every round
  - **Likelihood**: from round 1 on, the reconstruction runs on the clock branch lengths (`fn RoundState::marginal_update()` [packages/treetime/src/timetree/round.rs#L403-L407](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/round.rs#L403-L407)), and the substitution log-likelihood of the edge rises from $-187.6$ at zero length to $-140.59$ at 10 mutations and peaks at about 10.8 mutations ($-140.60$ at 12, $-141.1$ at 15, $-142.8$ at 20, $-148.0$ at 30, $-203$ at 100 mutations). In round 0 it peaks at zero length, and the tail fit gives 6.5 nats per year (5.9 without indels)
  - **Indel term**: the edge has no indel event, so the term is $-\mu b$ with $\mu = 2211$ per unit branch length, a decay of 0.55 nats per year. The hypothesis that the indel term makes the likelihood still rise at the end of the pilot grid is refuted
  - **Tail fit with indels**: in round 2 the decay moves the maximum of the sum two points inside the grid end (0 at the third last point, $5.3 \cdot 10^{-5}$ and $2.1 \cdot 10^{-4}$ at the last two). `fn SoftTailLaw::fit()` fits the flat top of the peak and returns the slope $2.9 \cdot 10^{-4}$ nats per year, while the likelihood falls by 2.9 nats over the next 10 mutations (1.3 years) and by about 8 nats per year beyond 100 mutations. The tail mass $p_\text{edge} / k$ of that slope puts the survival quantile at 26 180 years
  - **Tail fit at a rising end**: the maximum is the last grid point in rounds 1 and 2 without indels, and in round 1 with indels. The fitted slope rises, and the fit clamps it to flat. A flat tail has infinite mass, so `fn rewindow_to_mass()` returns the pilot grid unchanged [packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/mass_domain.rs#L36-L38](../../packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/mass_domain.rs#L36-L38), and the `--no-indels` run finishes with a flat right tail on this edge. Over all passes of the `--no-indels` run, 49 branch distributions had a flat right tail, and 16 a decaying slope below 1 nat per pilot width, with mass windows up to 337 years (10 040 points) for pilot grids of 1.4 to 10 years
  - **Wider pilot grid**: with the span $\max(5 b, 30 / L)$ in a temporary build, the run with indels completes, edge 733 gets a decaying tail, and `NODE_0000379` is dated 2021.104, against 2021.075 with `--no-indels`. Edges 288 and 312 (divergence lengths $3.2 \cdot 10^{-4}$ and $1.1 \cdot 10^{-4}$, pilot grids of $5 b$) still have a likelihood that rises at the end of the pilot grid, so a larger constant leaves the cause in place
  - **v0**: for a branch this short (mutation length below $\min(10^{-5}, 0.1 / L)$), `BranchLenInterpolator` samples 10 mutations linearly and then a quadratic grid up to `MAX_BRANCH_LENGTH` = 4.0 [packages/legacy/treetime/treetime/branch_len_interpolator.py#L38-L47](../../packages/legacy/treetime/treetime/branch_len_interpolator.py#L38-L47), so its grid holds a peak beyond 10 mutations

  Fix direction: build the pilot grid from the likelihood instead of the divergence length alone. Extend its upper end until the log-likelihood falls a set number of nats below its maximum, up to the largest branch length, so that the soft tail fit reads a decaying tail. The width of the peak and the clipping risk of the uniform pilot grid are the subject of [M-timetree-branch-grid-uniform-resolution.md](M-timetree-branch-grid-uniform-resolution.md). Validation: `scov-oc43` completes with indels, no branch distribution of that run or of the `--no-indels` run keeps a flat right tail, and its largest branch window stays within a small multiple of the span where the likelihood holds its mass

- **Soft window clamp refuted**: clamping the soft side of a mass window to a multiple of the input grid width failed 4 tests and moved flu dates by up to 13.5 years, because the far mass is real tail mass that the FFT drops later

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Decision required.** How to guard against a soft tail whose slope is near zero. Options:
>
> - **Lower bound on the slope of a soft tail law**, for example from the width of the distribution's grid: changes the mass of the tail, which the log marginal likelihood and the posterior statistics read, and needs a rule for the bound
> - **Window cap relative to the input grid**: a mass window ends at most a fixed multiple of the operand's width from its grid; the refuted clamp above is one such rule, so a cap needs a measurement that it changes no result where the tail mass is real
> - **Error that names the node**: stop with an error that names the node and the tail slope when a mass window exceeds a bound, instead of the grid point error
>
> Evidence: the cases above. The OC43 case comes from a pilot grid that ends at the likelihood peak, and a pilot grid that reaches past the peak removes it without a guard; none of the three guards fixes that cause, and an error would stop a run whose likelihood is valid. The narrow-branch case of [M-timetree-narrow-branch-distribution-forces-millions-of-grid-points.md](M-timetree-narrow-branch-distribution-forces-millions-of-grid-points.md) is a different mechanism (a fine spacing, not a wide window).
