# The outputs of timetree depend on the rounding of the platform

Differences in the last bits of linear algebra and math functions can grow through the time inference of `timetree` to relative differences in the clock rate above the project threshold of 1e-6. Changes of the OpenBLAS build have changed the clock rate by up to 1.7e-3 relative. Between the two OpenBLAS kernel sets of the current native build, the differences stay far below the threshold. `ancestral` gives byte-identical outputs on the CLI and in the WebAssembly back end of web2.

## Evidence

The current builds use OpenBLAS develop `de5d4914` on every target: natively with LAPACK compiled by flang 22, in web2 with the LAPACK C translation (the `openblas` entries of `.config/mise.toml` and `.config/mise.image.toml`).

Measured on the current code with this build, single-threaded, `timetree` with default settings and, for `data/zika/20`, the configuration that web2 resolves for a run with tree, alignment, and metadata (`--max-iter=2`, marginal branch lengths, `--clock-filter=3`, the default at the time of the measurement):

| Dataset | Output | CLI, OpenBLAS `Haswell` kernels | CLI, `OPENBLAS_CORETYPE=Prescott` (`Katmai` kernels) | Relative difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `zika/20` | `clock_rate` of `timetree.clock-model.json` | 0.0010255720768280348 | 0.001025572076832962 | 4.8e-12 |
| `zika/20` | `log_lh_time` of the first iteration (`tracelog.csv`) | -40.37081080491443 | -40.3708108065102 | 4.0e-11 |
| `zika/20` | `log_lh_seq` of the first iteration | -17281.96059624937 | -17281.96059625 | 3.6e-14 |
| `ebola/100` | `clock_rate` | 0.000728844005662043 | 0.0007288440068025652 | 1.6e-9 |
| `tb/20` | `clock_rate` | 0.0005375728626081336 | 0.0005375728625939621 | 2.6e-11 |

On `zika/20`, `timetree.nwk`, `timetree.nexus`, `timetree.auspice.json`, and `timetree.sequences.fasta` are byte-identical between the two kernel sets; `timetree.model.json`, `timetree.clock-model.json`, and `timetree.node-data.json` differ in the last digits. web2 is not measured with this build.

Earlier measurements show the amplification:

- With OpenBLAS 0.3.28 natively and OpenBLAS develop `eb00d597` in web2, `data/zika/20` with the web2 configuration gave clock rates 0.0010266268996163546 (`Haswell`), 0.0010266466684456434 (`Katmai`), and 0.001026646778721711 (web2, `WASM128_GENERIC`): 1.9e-5 relative between web2 and `Haswell`, and 1.1e-7 between web2 and `Katmai`. The log-likelihood of the sequences in the first iteration differed by 2.0e-7 relative between web2 and the CLI, and the root-to-tip divergence of the first tip by 1.2e-9 relative
- The OpenBLAS version alone changes `timetree` beyond the threshold. `dev/smoke` with the same code, once with OpenBLAS 0.3.28 (commit `2dabfc59`) and once with 0.3.34, both with `Haswell` kernels on an Intel i9-10900K: the clock rate changes by 9.2e-4 relative on `lassa/L/50`, 3.9e-4 on `ebola/362` with skyline, 3.2e-4 on `ebola/100`, 2.6e-4 on `tb/20`, and 7.9e-6 on `rsv/a/100`; the branch lengths of the time tree change by up to 0.4 on `lassa/L/50`, and the reconstructed sequences of `lassa/L/50` and of `ebola/362` with `--clock-filter=10` change. `timetree` on `zika/20` stays byte-identical. The outputs of `clock`, `homoplasy`, and `prune` stay byte-identical, and those of `ancestral`, `mugration`, and `optimize` change by at most 1.8e-7 relative, apart from the last printed digit of one value in `mugration.trait-probabilities.tsv`
- The OpenBLAS build changes `timetree` beyond the threshold again. `dev/smoke` (quick tier) of commit `2e275920` against `4f116c6d`, the same code once with the OpenBLAS 0.3.34 release of the earlier images, once with develop `de5d4914` and LAPACK compiled by flang 22, both with `Haswell` kernels on an Intel i9-10900K: `timetree` changes in 58 cases; the clock rate changes by 1.7e-3 relative on `ebola/20` with coalescent optimization, 1.5e-3 on `flu/h3n2/20` dense and with coalescent, 3.7e-4 on `dengue/20`, 3.4e-4 on `ebola/362` with skyline, 3.3e-4 on `ebola/100`, 3.2e-4 on `tb/20`, below 3e-4 on the other datasets, and by at most 2.4e-11 on most `flu/h3n2/20` variants. The other commands change by at most: `ancestral` 1.9e-12 relative (GTR of `--model-iterations=3`), `optimize` 4.3e-11 apart from one branch of 3.7e-8 on `ebola/100` (1.1e-5 relative, 4.2e-13 absolute), `mugration` 1.4e-7 apart from the last printed digit of one value in `mugration.trait-probabilities.tsv` and one tied state on `dengue/100`, which the tie rule of [kb/decisions/posterior-most-likely-state-tie-rule.md](../decisions/posterior-most-likely-state-tie-rule.md) now fixes. `clock`, `homoplasy`, and `prune` stay byte-identical. `cli/pipeline-steps-timetree` and `config/zika/20/pipeline` change through their `timetree` step
- `ancestral` on `data/flu/h3n2/20`: `node-data.json`, `model.json`, `nexus`, `nwk`, and `sequences.fasta` of web2 and of the CLI are byte-identical
- The compile-time CPU level of TreeTime's own code is not a rounding source: x86_64 builds with `-C target-cpu=haswell`, `x86-64-v3`, and `x86-64-v2` (liblzma and libzstd unchanged) wrote byte-identical outputs for the `flu/h3n2/500` pipeline, dense and sparse `ancestral`, `optimize`, `mugration`, and `timetree` on `lassa/L/500`, `rsv/a/2000`, and `flu/h3n2/500` with the skyline and confidence intervals, at 1 and at 20 threads. Rust does not fuse multiplications and additions into FMA without `mul_add`, which no TreeTime code calls
- Rounding sources that differ between the platforms: the OpenBLAS kernels (FMA in `Haswell`, none in `Katmai` and `WASM128_GENERIC`); LAPACK (OpenBLAS develop `de5d4914` compiled by flang 22 natively, its C translation with the LAPACK 3.9 `?lartg` and `?lassq` in web2); `exp`, `log`, and `pow` (glibc natively, with FMA variants on FMA CPUs; musl of Emscripten, from the same ARM optimized routines, in web2); the small products of `matrixmultiply` (FMA kernels natively, the fallback kernel in WebAssembly)

Measured with OpenBLAS `Haswell` against `Katmai` kernels on `rust` of 2026-10-24 at `9b10ffd3d`, which does not have the fix of the division ends of the next section (`0c5f1af62`), with default settings, `--seed 1`, single-threaded: the clock rates of the cases with the largest earlier changes agree within the threshold, with relative differences $1.2 \cdot 10^{-7}$ on `zika/20`, $1.6 \cdot 10^{-11}$ on `ebola/20` with `--coalescent opt`, $2 \cdot 10^{-13}$ on `flu/h3n2/20` dense with coalescent, and 0 on `lassa/L/50`. The node dates of `ebola/20` with `--coalescent opt` still differ by up to $6.9 \cdot 10^{-4}$ years, about $10^7$ times the rate difference, so an amplifying step remains; the other cases differ by at most $10^{-6}$ years.

## Amplifying steps

Random-rounding runs under Verrou (each floating-point operation rounded up or down at random, 12 runs per dataset and round, `--seed=1 --jobs=1`, compared with the nearest-rounding run, which gives the native result bit for bit) locate the steps of `timetree` that turn last-bit differences into differences above the threshold. A debug build recorded every discrete grid choice of the time inference, so the first discrete choice that differs from the nearest-rounding run marks the step that amplifies.

- **Spread of the clock rate** in an earlier Verrou trial with 20 random-rounding runs per dataset and no `--seed`, relative to the nearest-rounding run: `zika/20` 3.8e-4 (root date up to 2.5e-3 years), `ebola/20 --coalescent=opt` 2.4e-4, `flu/h3n2/20 --representation=dense` 1.9e-4, `lassa/L/50` 7.7e-11
- **Entry points of the rounding errors**: `verrou_dd_sym` on `zika/20` (clock rate compared within 1e-6 relative) found two independent minimal sets of functions, `ForwardPass::node` (`packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/forward_pass.rs`) and `multiply_functions::<NegLog>` (`packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/multiply.rs`); `verrou_dd_line` located float additions in both. These searches find where the rounding errors that matter enter, not the step that amplifies them
- **Coincident hard ends in divisions** (the largest effect, fixed): `fn division_side_tail()` in `packages/treetime-distribution/src/distribution_ops/divide.rs` gave a quotient a hard end only when the hard end of the dividend lay inside that of the divisor by an exact comparison. In the division of the parent posterior by the message to the parent (`fn refine_distribution_from_parent()` in `forward_pass.rs`), both ends are the same date and differ by at most 1 ulp after rounding. When the dividend end came out 1 ulp outside, the quotient got an `Error` tail, `fn operand_mass_domain()` in `edge_convolution.rs` found it not mass-sizable, and `fn convolution_output_window()` fell back to a window whose ends lie 0.8 to 3 years from those of the operand domains. The next product then had about 5 % more or fewer grid points, and the clock rate moved by up to 3.8e-4. `fn division_side_tail()` now treats hard ends that `coordinates_coincide()` finds equal (4 ulps of the larger magnitude) as inner ends. With this change, the largest change of the clock rate over 12 random-rounding runs fell from 3.8e-4 to 1.2e-7 on `zika/20` and from 1.9e-4 to 1.8e-10 on `flu/h3n2/20 --representation=dense`. The node time selection was not involved: no posterior had a grid neighbour within 1e-9 of its minimum
- **Grid dependence**: builds with 300, 600, and 900 message grid points (`GRID_POINTS`) give clock rates that differ from the 300-point result by 6.4e-4 and 1.0e-3 on `zika/20`, 3.0e-3 and 3.5e-3 on `flu/h3n2/20` dense, and 3.5e-3 and 3.1e-3 on `ebola/20 --coalescent=opt`, in line with the discretization error of [M-timetree-time-pass-posteriors-deviate-from-the-exact-posterior.md](M-timetree-time-pass-posteriors-deviate-from-the-exact-posterior.md). Each grid choice that a last-bit change can flip moves the results by a part of this error, so agreement within 1e-6 between platforms needs grid choices that are stable against last-bit changes; finer grids alone do not give it

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Investigation required.** Sensitive mass quantiles and tail fits on `ebola/20 --coalescent=opt`: with the fix of the division ends, 11 of 12 random-rounding runs still move the clock rate by more than 1e-6, up to 2.3e-4. The first differences are the grid point count of a forward message, whose resolving spacing (the mass width of the posterior between its `TRIM_EPS` quantiles divided by 299, `fn finer_spacing_needed()`) changes by 7e-4 relative, and the `EPS` quantile of the soft left tail of an operand, which moves by 3e-5 years while the 5-point fit of its slope changes by 2.4e-6 relative. The inputs at these points differ by about 1e-10, and the optimized T_c by up to 2.2e-5 relative. Find the input of the quantile that loses the precision (`MassProfile`, `SoftTailLaw::fit`) before a fix is chosen. Also find the step that amplifies the difference of the node dates of `ebola/20` with `--coalescent opt` between the `Haswell` and `Katmai` kernels (the kernel measurement above). That step and the sensitive quantiles may be one mechanism; no measurement has established it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Investigation required.** Measure web2 with the current build against the CLI on `data/zika/20` and on the datasets with the largest earlier changes (`ebola/20` with coalescent optimization, `flu/h3n2/20` dense, `lassa/L/50`). The core test suite can run in WebAssembly under Node (`cargo test --target wasm32-unknown-emscripten` with unwinding and a Node runner), which compares each step with its native result within the test tolerances.

The reproducibility target is equality within tolerances ([kb/decisions/reproducibility-across-platforms-within-tolerances.md](../decisions/reproducibility-across-platforms-within-tolerances.md)): the steps of `timetree` that amplify rounding differences beyond the threshold must be made stable.

## Validation

- The clock rate, the node dates, and the trees of `timetree` agree between the CLI with different OpenBLAS kernels and web2 within the agreed tolerance, or byte for byte
