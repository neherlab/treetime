# Timetree branch-length factor is mass-windowed at construction

`fn compute_branch_length_distribution()` evaluates the branch-length likelihood on a pilot grid, attaches its tail laws, and then resamples it onto a mass-bounded window with `fn rewindow_to_mass()` [packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L79](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L79). The stored branch-length distribution is therefore the re-windowed grid, not the pilot grid ([M-timetree-branch-grid-uniform-resolution.md](M-timetree-branch-grid-uniform-resolution.md) describes the pilot extent).

The branch-length factor is the likelihood that the branch-length optimizer evaluates, `fn evaluate_with_indels_log_lh_only()` [packages/treetime/src/optimize/likelihood.rs#L25](../../packages/treetime/src/optimize/likelihood.rs#L25), sampled at each pilot grid point by `fn evaluate_log_lh_on_grid()` [packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L292](../../packages/treetime/src/timetree/inference/branch_length_likelihood.rs#L292). It is the sum over partitions of the substitution-model log-likelihood of the branch, in which each site contributes the log of a sum of the exponentials of the rate eigenvalues times the branch length (`fn evaluate_dense_contribution()` [packages/treetime/src/optimize/dense_eval.rs#L6](../../packages/treetime/src/optimize/dense_eval.rs#L6), `fn evaluate_sparse_contribution()` [packages/treetime/src/optimize/sparse_eval.rs#L6](../../packages/treetime/src/optimize/sparse_eval.rs#L6)), plus a separate Poisson log-likelihood of the indel count of the branch (`fn IndelTerm.metrics()` [packages/treetime/src/optimize/indel.rs#L167](../../packages/treetime/src/optimize/indel.rs#L167)). Each site contributes its likelihood weighted by its multiplicity, a dot product of its coefficients with the exponentials of the rate eigenvalues times the branch length, floored at `f64::MIN_POSITIVE` (`fn evaluate_site_contributions()` and `fn site_likelihood()` [packages/treetime/src/optimize/eval.rs#L11-L64](../../packages/treetime/src/optimize/eval.rs#L11-L64)), so a site likelihood that underflows counts as $\ln$ `f64::MIN_POSITIVE` instead of $-\infty$. The shape of the stored factor depends on the inputs:

- Left side: `Hard` at length 0 when the log-likelihood is finite at zero length; when the indel term needs a positive length, the grid starts at 1% of one mutation and a `HardApproach` law toward length 0 is fitted (`fn branch_length_has_finite_boundary()` and `fn compute_branch_length_distribution()` in `branch_length_likelihood.rs`)
- Right side: a fitted soft tail when the likelihood has decayed by $\ln(1/\epsilon)$ below its maximum within the pilot grid or its extension, or when it is constant; `Hard` at `MAX_BRANCH_LENGTH` (5 substitutions per site) when it has not decayed by then (`fn pilot_end()`)
- Level: the costs are measured from the largest sampled log-likelihood (`fn peak_normalize_neg_log()`), so the cost 0 sits at the best grid point, not at the maximum-likelihood branch length, and that point can be an end of the grid, such as length 0 for a branch without changes

Neither the mass of the factor nor the extent of that mass has a closed form, so only evaluations of the likelihood can place a grid. The design of the timetree distributions assigns mass sizing to two consumers only: the output grid of a convolution across an edge, and the mass statistics of the posteriors (the 90% region and the survival functions). The construction of the branch-length factor is the one other place that mass-sizes a grid.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Decision required.** Keep the mass window at construction, or let the evaluated likelihood set the stored grid without a mass step.
>
> - Keep the window: the current behavior. One mass rule bounds every stored message, and the window cuts the pilot grid where it is wider than the mass of the likelihood, but a pilot grid that is coarse against the peak can blur the peak before the window measures mass ([M-timetree-branch-grid-uniform-resolution.md](M-timetree-branch-grid-uniform-resolution.md))
> - Grid from the likelihood: the extent and spacing follow from evaluations of the likelihood (its maximum, the curvature of the log-likelihood at the maximum, which `fn evaluate_with_indels()` returns with the log-likelihood, and the decay probes of `fn pilot_end()`), so the stored grid is the evaluated grid and no resample follows. It changes the branch-length grids of every timetree run, and the scientific change needs approval and an entry in `kb/decisions/`

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Investigation required.** No measurement shows how much the mass window moves node dates, or whether a grid without it moves them. Collect for both constructions: the node dates and the log marginal likelihood of the timetree smoke datasets, with the date changes in units of the node's 90% width, and the grid point counts per branch.
